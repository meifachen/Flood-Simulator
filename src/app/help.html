How to use the Flood Simulator

The simulator spreads water over the ground in two dimensions. The water can come from SWMM node flooding, rain on the grid, or lakes and other water bodies held at a level. Everything runs in this page: no files leave your computer, and nothing is installed.

1. Data

  • Ground (DEM): a single-band GeoTIFF in a projected coordinate system (feet or meters), north up. Clip and resample large lidar rasters first with python/prep_dem.py; the page accepts up to 40 million DEM cells and builds a grid of up to 8 million cells. If the file has no EPSG code, type it in.
  • Buildings: GeoJSON polygons. Cells whose centre falls inside a footprint become walls. Rain on a roof runs to the nearest ground cell. Longitude/latitude files are reprojected to the DEM's coordinate system.
  • Level boundaries: GeoJSON polygons for lakes, ponds or tidal water. Their level comes either from a SWMM storage or outfall node's head in the .out file, or from a fixed level you type (ft). A node or level_ft property fills this in automatically.
  • Land cover: a GeoTIFF of class codes (for example NLCD). Each cell takes the class at its centre. The Grid section lists the classes found, with editable Manning n and impervious %. NLCD starting values: n from Kalyanapu et al. (2009) Table 2 for the classes it lists; open water (11) 0.035 from Chow (1959) and cultivated crops (82) 0.06 from the SWMM manual's overland-flow table, since that paper gives none; impervious % from the midpoints of the NLCD developed classes. Check them for your site. The build reports a land cover or soils layer that covers none or only part of the grid.
  • Soils: GeoJSON polygons with an hsg property (A–D; a dual group such as B/D uses D), or a GeoTIFF of groups 1–4. Green-Ampt Ks, suction and initial moisture deficit (IMD) by group are editable. Ks and suction are the Rawls et al. (1983) texture values in the SWMM manual (A loamy sand, B silt loam, C sandy clay loam, D clay loam); the IMD is that table's porosity − field capacity, the deficit of a drained soil (A 0.332, B 0.217, C 0.154, D 0.154). An IMD drier than porosity − wilting point is flagged.
  • Inlets (two-way coupling): GeoJSON points with a node property naming the junction each inlet drains to, and optionally weir_length_ft, orifice_area_sqft, cw, co and rim_ft (sizes must be positive numbers). Each inlet takes water from the cell it sits in. Without rim_ft an inlet's crest is the SWMM rim of its node, the same crest a default inlet has; a crest more than 0.5 ft from the cell's ground, and an inlet more than 300 ft from its node, are reported. Points without a crs member are read as longitude/latitude when every coordinate fits in degrees (GeoJSON's standard), and reprojected.
  • SWMM5 .inp and .out: node coordinates must be in the DEM's coordinate system. Each node that floods in the .out file adds its flooding rate (cfs) to the ground cell at that node. A node on a building is moved to the nearest open cell within 30 ft.

2. Grid and surface

The cell size is a whole multiple of the DEM resolution; ground levels are block averages. Each cell is part impervious (no infiltration) and part pervious. Roughness and imperviousness come from the land cover, and Green-Ampt values from the soils, where those are loaded; the global values apply elsewhere. Loading a new DEM clears the other layers, so nothing from an earlier site is used by mistake.

3. Water sources

SWMM node flooding is the usual choice for a SWMM study: SWMM routes the subcatchment runoff through the pipes, and the water that leaves the system at surcharged nodes spreads on the ground. Rain on the grid adds rain to every cell; together with SWMM flooding it counts runoff twice wherever the grid overlaps SWMM subcatchments. Times are hours from the SWMM start date and time.

SWMM coupling:

  • One way reads the node flooding from your .out file. Water on the ground never drains back, so street ponding is an upper bound.
  • Two way runs EPA SWMM 5.2.4 inside the page and exchanges water every coupling interval (30 s by default):
    • overflow at a node spreads on the ground;
    • water standing over a junction goes back in through its inlet: the smaller of a weir flow on the depth over the crest and an orifice flow, whose head is that depth while the node is below the crest and the 2D level minus the node head when it is surcharged (so the two meet at the crest);
    • an inlet asks for the rate those equations give at the start of the interval; each engine step it takes that rate, or the water left in its cell if that is less, and SWMM is sent the volume actually taken. Capture therefore does not depend on the cell size. Node overflow from one interval is spread on the ground during the next;
    • each inlet in the inlets file takes water from its own cell; junctions without a listed inlet get the default inlet at the manhole, or none, as you choose; a junction whose listed inlets were all rejected gets none;
    • when the 2D run ends before SWMM's, the inlet inflow is set back to zero and SWMM runs on to its end, so its report holds only the water the inlets took;
    • ALLOW_PONDING is set to NO in the copy SWMM runs;
    • the SWMM report and results of the coupled run can be downloaded after the run.

4. Run and results

The engine uses the GPU (WebGPU) when the browser offers it, and the CPU otherwise. Stop ends a run and keeps the maps computed so far. The map and 3D views show the depth at the selected time or the maximum over the run. Two more views show the maximum velocity and the maximum depth × velocity. They are recorded where the water is deeper than 0.05 ft, and their color classes are for display, not a hazard standard. The solver caps every face at critical flow (Froude 1); where that cap acted, the cells are hatched in these views, counted in the KPI and marked in band 6 of the GeoTIFF: the speed there is a lower bound (on steep, smooth streets it can be 20–50% low), while depth × velocity is not affected. Click the map to see the ground level, the depth over time and the velocity maxima at a point.

Exports: a six-band GeoTIFF in the DEM's coordinate system (maximum depth, maximum water level, time of the maximum, maximum velocity, maximum depth × velocity, 1 where the speed was capped at critical flow), building maxima (CSV) and the SWMM exchange (CSV). Save results writes a .fsim.gz file with every map; Open saved results shows it again, with the same maps, charts, exports and panel settings, without the input files. Results cannot be opened while a run is going, and Run stays off until a DEM or an example is loaded.

Method

Local-inertial shallow-water equations on a square grid (Bates et al. 2010; de Almeida et al. 2012, θ = 0.8). Friction is semi-implicit (Manning). Flow per unit width is capped at the critical (Froude 1) value, and an outflow limiter keeps depths non-negative. The time step follows the Courant condition, sized for the deepest water expected in each interval, including what a point inflow or rain can add before the next reading. Green-Ampt infiltration uses the SWMM integrated form with ponded depth in the suction term. Open grid edges let water leave at normal depth. A water balance is kept at every map, and its closure is shown.

Limits

  • Two-way coupling is explicit (one coupling interval of lag).
  • Cell velocity is the mean of the two face flows in each direction divided by the depth; it is a cell-average value.
  • The local-inertial method is for subcritical flow: speeds above critical are capped (see the hatched cells).
  • Building heights in 3D are assumed from footprint area unless the file has a height property.
  • Coordinates are converted with proj4 without datum grid shifts, so the basemap can be off by a meter or so; the model itself is unaffected.
