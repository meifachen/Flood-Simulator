1. Quick start

The simulator spreads water over the ground in two dimensions. The water can come from SWMM node flooding, rain on the grid, or lakes and other water bodies held at a level. Everything runs in this page: no files leave your computer, and nothing is installed. It works offline; only the OpenStreetMap and USGS basemaps need the internet.

  1. Load the example. Under 1. Data, pick an example and press Load. The example fills in the DEM, buildings, the pond, the SWMM model and its own run settings.
  2. Check the grid. Under 2. Grid and surface, the cell size is set (12 ft for the US example). A smaller cell is more detailed and slower: halving the cell size takes about eight times as long.
  3. Check the water. Under 3. Water sources, the example uses SWMM node flooding from its .out file (one way) between 0.5 and 1.5 hours.
  4. Run. Under 4. Run, press Run. The bar and the message show progress; Stop keeps the maps made so far.
  5. Read the results. Move the time slider or press Play; choose Maximum depth under Show; click the map for the depth over time at a point; read the water balance under the charts.

For your own site, load a DEM first (it sets the coordinate system and the grid), then the optional layers in any order, choose the water sources and run.

2. Workflow map, tooltips and tour

  • Workflow map (at the top of the page): the five steps from data to results, each with the items that belong to it. A step turns green when it is done, and the next step to do is outlined. Click a step to open its panel; click an item to jump to that setting; click Guide to read about it here.
  • Tooltips: hover over, or tab to, any setting, button or result figure for what it is, its units, its default and its allowed range. On a phone, tap the field; the tip closes by itself.
  • Tour: the Tour button in the header loads the example and walks through one run in eight short steps. It offers itself once, on the first visit in a browser; it can be skipped at any step.

4. Grid and surface

The cell size is a whole multiple of the DEM resolution; ground levels are block averages. Each cell is part impervious (no infiltration) and part pervious. Roughness and imperviousness come from the land cover, and Green-Ampt values from the soils, where those are loaded; the global values below apply elsewhere.

5. Water sources

Use at least one: SWMM coupling, rain on the grid or a level boundary. Times are hours from the SWMM start date and time (or from 0 without SWMM).

  • Off: no SWMM water.
  • One way: reads each node's flooding from your .out file and adds it to the ground cell at that node. Water on the ground never drains back, so street ponding is an upper bound. This is the usual choice when SWMM already routes the subcatchment runoff.
  • Two way: runs EPA SWMM 5.2.4 inside the page and exchanges water every coupling interval (30 s by default). Overflow at a node spreads on the ground; water over an inlet goes back into SWMM (section 6). ALLOW_PONDING is set to NO in the copy SWMM runs. When the 2D run ends before SWMM's, the inlet inflow is set back to zero and SWMM runs on to its end, so its report holds only the water the inlets took. The SWMM report and results of the coupled run can be downloaded.
  • Rain on the grid: from a SWMM rain gauge (intensity, volume or cumulative) or uniform. Together with SWMM subcatchments it counts the runoff twice wherever the grid overlaps them; the page warns when the SWMM model has subcatchments on that gauge. A two-way run with no rain on the grid is refused when the SWMM model has no subcatchments or inflows of its own, since it would have no water.
  • Map every: how often a map is kept (minutes). More maps make smoother playback and a larger results file.

6. Inlets (two-way coupling)

An inlet takes water from the cell it sits in and passes it to its SWMM junction. Before each coupling interval it asks for the rate its equations give at the depth there; each engine step it takes that rate, or the water left in its cell if that is less, and SWMM is sent the volume actually taken. Capture therefore does not depend on the cell size. Node overflow from one interval spreads on the ground in the next.

Junctions without a listed inlet get the default inlet below at the manhole, or take no water, as you choose. A junction whose listed inlets were all rejected gets none. The crest of an inlet is its rim_ft, else its node's SWMM rim; a crest more than 0.5 ft from the cell's ground, and an inlet more than 300 ft from its node, are reported.

7. Level boundaries and lakes

Cells inside a level-boundary polygon hold water at the boundary's level. Water that runs into them leaves the 2D surface, and they can spill onto land below their level. The table under the boundaries file sets, for each polygon, where its level comes from:

  • Fixed level (ft), typed in or from the polygon's level_ft.
  • A SWMM storage or outfall node: its head from the .out file, or live in two-way coupling.
  • Routed: the lake starts at the level given and rises and falls with the water it gains: the water the 2D sends into it, rain on its whole area and, in two-way coupling, the inflow of SWMM outfalls that discharge into it. Those outfalls take the lake level as their stage. The lake is a level pool with a constant area and no outlet. Give its whole surface area in Lake area (or an area_ac property) when the grid holds only part of it; blank uses the part inside the grid. A routed property of true selects this when the file loads. The results show each routed lake's start, peak and end level and where its water came from.

9. Results

  • Map views (Show): depth at the selected time, maximum depth, maximum velocity, maximum depth × velocity, and, after a comparison, the change in maximum depth. Velocities are recorded where the water is deeper than 0.05 ft; their color classes are for display, not a hazard standard.
  • Critical-speed hatching: the solver caps every face at critical flow (Froude 1). Where that cap acted, cells are hatched in the velocity views, counted in the KPI and marked in band 6 of the GeoTIFF: the speed there is a lower bound (on steep, smooth streets it can be 20–50% low), while depth × velocity is not affected.
  • 3D: ground, buildings, water and SWMM nodes; drag to rotate, right-drag to pan, scroll to zoom; the vertical exaggeration can be set.
  • Charts: flooded area over time, the SWMM exchange at nodes (the seven largest, the rest as Other), level-boundary levels, and the depth at the clicked point.
  • Map click: ground level, depth over time and the velocity maxima at that point.
  • KPIs: the figures above the map; hover for their definitions.

10. Water balance

Every map closes a balance of the water on the surface. Positive terms add water.

  • Rain on the grid, SWMM node flooding: water added.
  • Taken by SWMM inlets: what the inlets took (demand the cells could not supply is not counted).
  • Infiltration, Out at the grid edges, Into level boundaries: water removed (a negative level-boundary term means water spilled out of a lake).
  • Change in water on the surface; Depth clamped at zero: a numerical correction, normally tiny.
  • Closure should be about 0; the note gives it as a share of the water moved.

In two-way runs a second table gives the SWMM side: overflow onto the 2D, water into inlets, overflow at uncoupled nodes (lost), inlet demand the surface could not supply, overflow made in the last interval (after the 2D ended) and SWMM's own continuity error.

11. Save, export and compare

  • GeoTIFF: six bands in the DEM's coordinate system: maximum depth, maximum water level, time of the maximum, maximum velocity, maximum depth × velocity, and 1 where the speed was capped at critical flow.
  • Buildings (CSV): the deepest water next to each footprint. SWMM inflows (CSV): the exchange at each node over time.
  • Save results: a .fsim.gz file with every map. Open saved results (under Data) shows it again, with the same maps, charts, exports and panel settings, without the input files.
  • Compare with saved results: opens another saved run on the same grid (same DEM and cell size) and maps the change in maximum depth, this run minus that one: newly flooded, no longer flooded, deeper and shallower (by 0.1 ft or more), and buildings reached in each. Runs on different grids are refused. Use it for before and after a project, or for two lake-level assumptions.

13. Method

Local-inertial shallow-water equations on a square grid (Bates et al. 2010; de Almeida et al. 2012, θ = 0.8). Friction is semi-implicit (Manning). Flow per unit width is capped at the critical (Froude 1) value, and an outflow limiter keeps depths non-negative. The time step follows the Courant condition, sized for the deepest water expected in each interval, including what a point inflow or rain can add before the next reading. Green-Ampt infiltration uses the SWMM integrated form with ponded depth in the suction term. Open grid edges let water leave at normal depth. A water balance is kept at every map, and its closure is shown.

14. Limits

  • Two-way coupling is explicit (one coupling interval of lag).
  • Inlets use sag equations at the local depth; on-grade interception curves are not used.
  • A routed lake is a level pool with a constant area and no outlet.
  • A routed lake's level is updated once per chunk of 2D steps, from the water that crossed its edge in that chunk. Chunks are kept short while the level moves, so it ends where a fine update ends (within 0.02 ft in the tests); but when a lake suddenly spills onto dry land, its lowest level can be off by a few tenths of a foot for a short time.
  • Peak depths during fast changes depend somewhat on the 2D time step (the Courant number); a lower Courant number gives a closer peak at more cost.
  • Cell velocity is the mean of the two face flows in each direction divided by the depth; it is a cell-average value.
  • The local-inertial method is for subcritical flow: speeds above critical are capped (see the hatched cells).
  • Building heights in 3D are assumed from footprint area unless the file has a height property.
  • Coordinates are converted with proj4 without datum grid shifts, so the basemap can be off by a meter or so; the model itself is unaffected.

15. Terms

  • DEM: digital elevation model, a raster of ground levels.
  • Level boundary: cells held at a water level (lake, pond, tide).
  • Coupling interval: how often SWMM and the 2D model exchange water in two-way runs.
  • Crest: the level above which water enters an inlet.
  • Surcharged: a node whose head is above its crest.
  • Courant number: the time step as a share of the time a shallow-water wave takes to cross a cell.
  • Froude number: speed divided by the shallow-water wave speed; 1 is critical flow.
  • HSG: hydrologic soil group (A drains fastest, D slowest).
